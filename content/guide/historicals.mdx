---
title: Historicals
description: What to pull from the financial statements before projecting anything.
---

Before forecasting anything, pull the last 3 to 5 years of the company's revenue, EBITDA margin,
capex, net working capital, and debt straight from its annual reports. Not because the past
repeats itself, it rarely does, but because it's the only evidence you have for whether your
forecast assumptions are even plausible. If a company's margin has sat between 18% and 22% for
five straight years, forecasting 35% from next year onward needs a real reason, not a hope.

The one trap worth knowing is that reported numbers aren't always "normal" numbers. A company
that sold a factory last year will show an unusually large one-off gain in its profit; one that
wrote down a bad investment will show an unusually large loss. Imagine judging someone's monthly
salary from a month they also won a lottery ticket. You'd wildly overestimate their normal
income. Strip these one-offs out before you use the historical trend for anything.

## What this guide skips

This guide's running example starts straight from a Year 0 base revenue of ₹1,000 cr instead of
building that number up from real filings. The real models in [/models](/models) show that part
with actual company data.
