---
title: WACC
description: The discount rate, cost of equity via CAPM, blended with after-tax cost of debt.
---

WACC answers one question. What return does this specific business need to generate to be worth
tying your money up in? A government bond is about as safe as money gets, so it pays a
low return, the risk-free rate. A random small company might double or go to zero, so an
investor demands a much higher expected return to bother. CAPM is just a formula for sliding
along that scale. Start at the risk-free rate, then add a premium sized by `beta`, how much more
(or less) the stock swings than the market as a whole. A beta of 1.0 means "swings exactly with
the market," above 1.0 means more violently than the market, below means more calmly.

Debt and equity get blended because a real business is funded by both lenders and shareholders,
and each has their own required return. One asymmetry is worth noticing. Interest paid to lenders
is tax-deductible for the company, so the after-tax cost of debt is lower than what it looks like
on paper, while dividends to shareholders get no such break. That's why `costOfDebt × (1 - taxRate)`
shows up below but cost of equity doesn't.

There's a genuine chicken-and-egg problem hiding here, worth flagging rather than hiding. WACC is
weighted using the company's *current market* value of equity and debt. But the whole point of
building a DCF is often to argue the current market price is wrong. If it's wrong, the weighting
is technically wrong too. In practice this is a second-order effect for most companies, but it's
the sort of thing a sharp reader should notice is circular, not swept under the rug.

## Worked example

**Cost of equity (CAPM)**

```
costOfEquity = riskFreeRate + beta × equityRiskPremium
             = 7% + 1.0 × 6%
             = 13%
```

**WACC**

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Market cap (equity weight) | ₹1,800 cr |
| Total debt (debt weight) | ₹200 cr |
| Cost of equity | 13% |
| Cost of debt | 8% |
| Tax rate | 25% |

```
weightOfEquity = 1,800 / (1,800 + 200) = 90%
weightOfDebt   = 200 / (1,800 + 200)   = 10%
afterTaxCostOfDebt = 8% × (1 - 25%) = 6%

WACC = 13% × 90% + 6% × 10%
     = 12.3%
```

Computed by `wacc()` in [`/lib/dcf.ts`](https://github.com/rishitjain2609/dcf-lab/blob/main/lib/dcf.ts).
