## Conventions used throughout this guide

- Currency: ₹ crore, unless stated otherwise.
- Explicit forecast period: 5 years.
- Discounting convention: end-year by default; mid-year is covered separately in
  [Discounting (mid-year)](/guide/discounting-mid-year).
- Risk-free rate: the 10-year Indian G-sec yield.
- A single running example carries through every step in this guide — a hypothetical company,
  not a real one. The two real models are in [/models](/models).

## The running example — starting assumptions

| Assumption | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Base revenue (Year 0) | ₹1,000 cr |
| Revenue growth, Years 1–5 | 10% every year |
| EBITDA margin | 20% |
| D&A, % of revenue | 5% |
| Capex, % of revenue | 6% |
| Δ NWC, % of revenue increase | 10% |
| Tax rate | 25% |
| Risk-free rate (10-yr G-sec) | 7% |
| Beta | 1.0 |
| Equity risk premium | 6% |
| Cost of debt | 8% |
| Market cap (for WACC weights) | ₹1,800 cr |
| Total debt (for WACC weights) | ₹200 cr |
| Net debt (for the equity bridge) | ₹200 cr |
| Diluted shares | 40 cr |
| Terminal growth | 4% |

Every assumption is flat and round on purpose — the point of this guide is to see how the
mechanics fit together, not to memorize a realistic set of numbers. Real companies rarely have the
same growth rate five years running.

Every number in the steps that follow is computed from this table using
[`/lib/dcf.ts`](https://github.com/rishitjain2609/dcf-lab/blob/main/lib/dcf.ts) — not typed in by
hand — so it stays consistent end to end.

## Glossary

New to this? Keep this tab open — every later step links back here.

| Term | In plain English |
| --- | --- |
| **EBITDA** | Profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — operating profit before non-cash accounting items. |
| **EBIT** | EBITDA minus D&A. Closer to real operating profit, since depreciation is a real economic cost even though no cash leaves that year. |
| **NOPAT** | EBIT after tax, assuming the company had no debt — "what the business earns for everyone who's owed money or owns a piece of it." |
| **UFCF** | Unlevered free cash flow — the actual cash the business throws off each year, before paying interest to lenders. What gets discounted. |
| **WACC** | Weighted Average Cost of Capital — the discount rate; roughly, the return investors demand for the risk of this business. |
| **Terminal value** | The value of every cash flow after the explicit forecast window (Year 6 onward), collapsed into one number as of Year 5. |
| **Enterprise value (EV)** | The value of the whole operating business — what it would cost to buy the company outright, debt and all. |
| **Equity value** | What's left for shareholders after lenders are paid — EV minus net debt. What a share price is actually valuing. |
| **Implied value** | What this model says a share is worth under its assumptions. Not a prediction, not a "target price" — see the [disclaimer](/about). |
