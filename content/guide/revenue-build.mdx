---
title: Revenue build
description: Compounding growth assumptions into a 5-year revenue forecast.
---

This step is just compound interest wearing a business suit. If revenue grows 10% a year, next
year's revenue is this year's times 1.10, the same math as a savings account earning 10%
interest, just applied to sales instead of money sitting in a bank.

The judgment call is whether a single growth rate is even the right tool. A company with one
product in one market can often be modeled this way. A company selling three different product
lines into five different countries, growing at different speeds, really should be modeled
segment by segment. A single blended growth rate can hide a profitable segment shrinking while a
low-margin one grows, and the total still looks fine on the top line.

## Worked example

Starting from ₹1,000 cr, growing 10% every year.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue (₹ cr) | 1,100.00 | 1,210.00 | 1,331.00 | 1,464.10 | 1,610.51 |

```
revenue[t] = revenue[t-1] × (1 + growth)
1,100.00 = 1,000.00 × 1.10
1,210.00 = 1,100.00 × 1.10
```

Computed by `projectUnleveredFreeCashFlow` in [`/lib/dcf.ts`](/guide/ufcf).
