---
title: EV-to-equity bridge
description: Enterprise value isn't what a shareholder owns — this step gets to equity value and price per share.
---

Think of buying a house with a mortgage still on it. The house itself might be worth ₹1 crore —
that's its enterprise value, what the whole asset is worth regardless of who's financed it. But
if ₹30 lakh of mortgage is still outstanding, the seller doesn't walk away with ₹1 crore; they
walk away with ₹70 lakh, because the bank gets paid first. Equity value works the same way:
lenders have first claim on everything the business is worth, and whatever's left after they're
paid is what belongs to shareholders.

Two smaller adjustments can apply on top of net debt: **minority interest** subtracts out value
that technically belongs to minority shareholders of a subsidiary the company doesn't fully own
(it shows up in the parent's financials, but isn't fully the parent's to claim), and
**investments** adds back the value of assets the company holds — like a stake in another company
— that generate real value but were never part of the UFCF forecast in the first place.

> New to these terms? See the [glossary](/guide/setup-and-conventions#glossary).

## Worked example

Using the end-year enterprise value from [Discounting (mid-year)](/guide/discounting-mid-year) —
₹439.12 cr of explicit FCFs plus ₹1,055.38 cr of discounted terminal value:

| Step | ₹ cr |
| --- | --- |
| Enterprise value | 1,494.50 |
| − Net debt | 200.00 |
| **Equity value** | **1,294.50** |
| ÷ Diluted shares (cr) | 40 |
| **Implied value per share** | **₹32.36** |

```
equityValue = enterpriseValue - netDebt - minorityInterest + investments
impliedValuePerShare = equityValue / dilutedShares
```

Using the mid-year enterprise value instead (₹1,583.75 cr) gives an implied value per share of
₹34.59 — a reminder that the discounting convention alone can move the answer by a few percent.
