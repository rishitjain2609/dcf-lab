---
title: Unlevered free cash flow
description: NOPAT, plus D&A, minus capex, minus the increase in net working capital.
---

"Unlevered" just means this cash flow pretends the company has no debt at all — notice there's no
interest expense anywhere in the table below. That sounds like it's ignoring something important,
but it's deliberate: it means two companies that make identical products and sell identical
amounts, but happen to be financed differently (one took on a big loan, one didn't), end up with
the *same* unlevered free cash flow. The financing decision gets factored back in later, in the
discount rate (WACC) and the debt subtracted in the equity bridge — not buried inside the cash
flow itself. That separation is what lets a DCF compare a heavily-indebted company to a debt-free
one on equal footing.

> New to these terms? See the [glossary](/guide/setup-and-conventions#glossary).

## Worked example

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EBIT | 165.00 | 181.50 | 199.65 | 219.62 | 241.58 |
| NOPAT (× (1 − 25% tax)) | 123.75 | 136.13 | 149.74 | 164.71 | 181.18 |
| + D&A | 55.00 | 60.50 | 66.55 | 73.21 | 80.53 |
| − Capex (6% of revenue) | 66.00 | 72.60 | 79.86 | 87.85 | 96.63 |
| − Δ NWC (10% of revenue increase) | 10.00 | 11.00 | 12.10 | 13.31 | 14.64 |
| **UFCF** | **102.75** | **113.03** | **124.33** | **136.76** | **150.44** |

```
nopat[t]  = ebit[t] × (1 - taxRate)
ufcf[t]   = nopat[t] + da[t] - capex[t] - nwcChange[t]
```
