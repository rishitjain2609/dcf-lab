---
title: Terminal value (Gordon + exit multiple)
description: Valuing every cash flow after the explicit forecast window, two different ways.
---

No one can credibly forecast a company's cash flows year by year for the next fifty years — the
uncertainty compounds into nonsense after about five. But the company (assuming it survives)
keeps generating cash well past year five. Terminal value is how a DCF deals with that: instead
of forecasting years 6, 7, 8... forever, it collapses "everything from year 6 onward" into a
single number, valued as of the end of year 5.

This is worth sitting with, because it's usually the most important number in the whole model:
in the running example on this site, roughly 70% of total value comes from the terminal value,
not from the five years of cash flow actually forecast in detail. A DCF can look exhaustively
researched — five years of careful revenue and margin assumptions — while most of its answer
actually rests on one growth-rate assumption about forever.

Two standard ways to build it: the **Gordon growth** method below treats the business as a
perpetuity growing at a constant rate forever (like a rental property that keeps paying rent
every year, growing slightly with inflation). The **exit multiple** method instead asks "what
would a buyer pay for this business in year 5," using a multiple of EBITDA or revenue the way
you'd price a house by comparing it to similar houses that recently sold nearby.

> New to these terms? See the [glossary](/guide/setup-and-conventions#glossary).

## Gordon growth method

```
TV = UFCF_year5 × (1 + terminalGrowth) / (WACC - terminalGrowth)
   = 150.44 × 1.04 / (12.3% - 4%)
   = 1,884.98
```

The guard: `terminalGrowth` must be strictly less than `WACC`, or the formula divides by a
negative number and the terminal value comes out negative — a sign that something upstream is
wrong, not a valid result. `terminalValueGordon()` in
[`/lib/dcf.ts`](https://github.com/rishitjain2609/dcf-lab/blob/main/lib/dcf.ts) throws rather than
return it.

## Exit multiple method

Instead of a growth-forever formula, pick a multiple a buyer might realistically pay for a
similar business in year 5 — say 10× EV/EBITDA, a reasonable ballpark for a steady,
moderate-growth company (hot sectors trade richer, mature ones trade cheaper):

```
TV = EBITDA_year5 × multiple
   = 322.10 × 10
   = 3,221.00
```

That's almost double the Gordon growth answer of 1,884.98 from the same year-5 numbers — and
that gap is the whole point of running both. When two independent methods for the same thing
disagree this much, it's a signal to go back and ask which assumption is doing the unrealistic
work: is 10× too rich a multiple for this business, or is the Gordon growth formula's
`WACC − terminalGrowth` spread (12.3% − 4% = 8.3 points) too thin, making it overly sensitive?
Neither method is "the correct one" — the disagreement itself is useful information.

`terminalValueExitMultiple()` in
[`/lib/dcf.ts`](https://github.com/rishitjain2609/dcf-lab/blob/main/lib/dcf.ts) is just
`finalMetric × multiple`.
