## What a DCF is

TODO(Harsh): plain-language definition — a DCF values a business as the sum of the cash it will
generate in the future, discounted back to today at a rate that reflects how risky those cash
flows are.

## Where it's used

TODO(Harsh): cover the main real-world uses:

- Equity research — analysts publish an implied value to justify a rating.
- M&A — buyers use a DCF to sanity-check what they're paying relative to what the target can
  generate.
- Private equity — underwriting a deal against a hold period and exit assumption.
- IPO pricing — bankers and the company triangulate a price range partly off a DCF.

## Where it breaks

TODO(Harsh): the honest limits — most of a DCF's value usually sits in the terminal value, which
is extremely sensitive to small changes in WACC and terminal growth; forecast error compounds over
a 5–10 year explicit period; and a DCF can be reverse-engineered to justify almost any price if the
assumptions are chosen after the fact rather than before. This is also why [/ledger](/ledger)
exists — the test of a DCF isn't how it looks today, it's whether it was right.
